## Why GurtOS, and what each part is for

**The problem.** A company will not let an agent near its books while the agent runs as its owner, with the owner's
keys, no separation of duties, no record it can show an auditor and no way to undo one thing. That is why finance
pilots stay pilots. This page is about whether a real company could switch the desk on. How well the desk decides is
on the [Hyphenate page](/tracks/hyphenate/).

**GurtOS Business is the answer for a company:** seats with their own users, approvals routed to the right person, a
full audit and a kill switch, on a box the company owns. Business is on main. The screens below are from a box seeded as the made-up Fogline Supply; the scored credit desk runs were on GurtOS Server, where the controller seat approves from the portal or a phone. The OS
is light (one Node service, 4 GB of memory) and open source under Apache-2.0, so a company can read what it runs.

## The Customer Impact Award

The customer is the controller at a small business. Today the desk stops the one application that would have cost
$20,000 of credit and brings only the decisions that need a person to a phone, with the reason and the data attached.
The measured-impact feature (being built) will put human minutes, dollars stopped and cost per application on the
same page, from the log, so the award case is a number the controller can check and not a claim. Fogline Supply Co. is
made up and no money moves.

## Real attacks on financial companies

A company that switches an agent on has to ask what an insider or a third-party tool could reach. On an ordinary operating system an agent doing this work holds the keys, can approve its own work and can pull any report. Real attacks on financial companies show why that matters. Each row names its source and what was taken as the source states it, then says what GurtOS's controls do about that kind of attack and what they do not.

| Incident | What was taken, and how | Source | What GurtOS does about this kind of attack | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coinbase, May 2025 | Coinbase's SEC filing says an attacker appears to have paid contractors or employees in support roles outside the United States to copy data from internal systems they could reach for their jobs. The data included names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, masked Social Security numbers and bank account numbers, government ID images, balance snapshots and transaction history. No passwords or private keys were taken and no one could reach customer funds. Coinbase estimated $180 million to $400 million to remediate and reimburse. | [Coinbase Form 8-K, May 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1679788/000167978825000094/coin-20250514.htm) | A seat holds only the tools its task lists, every call it makes goes in an append-only log with who asked and who approved, and an amount over a limit needs a second person. With Business, an auditor reads the whole record. | A person with root on the box can still edit the log, which the known limits list, and an insider with a legitimate right to customer data is a people problem the OS can only record. |
| TransUnion, July 2025 | Names, dates of birth and Social Security numbers of more than 4.4 million people, taken on July 28 from an outside application that held customer data for its U.S. consumer support. TransUnion says no credit information was accessed. The attacker is unknown and TransUnion declined to give more. | [TechCrunch, Aug 28, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/28/transunion-says-hackers-stole-4-4-million-customers-personal-information/) | A tool that talks to an outside service runs in the core with the key held there, and its task reaches only the hosts it is allowed. Anything large or unusual waits for a person's OK. | It does not stop a person from being talked into giving an outside party access. |

The merge gate, `./demo`, runs the whole box on a fresh test VM before anything reaches main. The Oct 24 run passed
22 sections and 175 checks with 0 failures, in 102 seconds on the x86 test VM. The parts this agent leans on:

## How it works

<Flow steps={frontmatter.flow} />

**Why the box is the path to production.** A finance agent that runs as a script on someone's laptop has the
laptop's keys, can write anywhere, and leaves no record a controller can audit. On GurtOS the same agent:

| Production need | What GurtOS does |
|---|---|
| Least privilege | Each seat is its own Linux user with no sudo, its own copy of the files, and only its job's tools. Segregation of duties is tool access. |
| Secrets | CRS and Nessie keys live in a root-only vault. Tools that need them run in the box's core; the seats get results. |
| Human approval | The tool returns "needs controller"; the box pauses only that task and sends the question to the portal and phone. |
| Audit | One append-only, fsynced action log: every call, result, approval and cost. Decisions carry rule ids and CRS request ids. |
| Reliability | After a power cut the box rebuilds every task from the log and resumes it. Updates install to a second system slot and roll back if unhealthy. |
| Change control | The rules are a YAML file. Swap it per run with `{"rules": "/path/to/rules.yaml"}`; the tools refuse rule ids it does not have. |
| Regression testing | Every run is scored against an answer key from its log, so a prompt or rules change is measured, not guessed. |

## Try it

On a box with `CRS_USERNAME`, `CRS_PASSWORD` and `NESSIE_API_KEY` in its vault:

```sh
curl -s -X POST localhost:7700/t/credit-desk/run -d '{"case": "fogline-2026-10"}'
curl -s localhost:7700/approvals
curl -s -X POST localhost:7700/approvals/<id> -d '{"approved": true, "by": "controller"}'
curl -s -X POST localhost:7700/t/credit-desk/score -d '{"run": "<run>"}'
```

Any case folder that passes `check-case.ts` runs the same way with `{"dir": "/path/to/case"}`. The Hyphenate page
has the steps for writing your own.

## Limits

- The scorer checks decisions field by field, but a finding counts as caught by matching its rows, wording and rule
  ids, not by checking that its conclusion is right.
- Two collection steps differ from the key in every run: the clerk put two over-limit customers on credit hold
  (rule COL-3) instead of the aging step the key lists.
- The business credit file is simulated, and the bank is read, not written.
- The scored runs used a single controller seat on GurtOS Server. Routing by amount to different people is in GurtOS Business, shown here on a seeded box with scripted seats and sandbox data, not in a scored run.
- Sign-in with work accounts (Entra ID) and shadow mode are not built; the log's `who` comes from a person's paired phone.
