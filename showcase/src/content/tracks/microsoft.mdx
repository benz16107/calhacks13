## Why GurtOS, and what each part is for

**The problem.** A company will not let an agent near its books while the agent runs as its owner, with the owner's
keys, no separation of duties, no record it can show an auditor and no way to undo one thing. That is why finance
pilots stay pilots. This page is about whether a real company could switch the desk on. How well the desk decides is
on the [Hyphenate page](/tracks/hyphenate/).

**GurtOS Business is the answer for a company:** seats with their own users, approvals routed to the right person, a
full audit and a kill switch, on a box the company owns. Business is on main. The screens below are from a box seeded as the made-up Fogline Supply; the scored credit desk runs were on GurtOS Server, where the controller seat approves from the portal or a phone. The OS
is light (one Node service, 4 GB of memory) and open source under Apache-2.0, so a company can read what it runs.

## How it works

<Flow steps={frontmatter.flow} />

**Why the box is the path to production.** A finance agent that runs as a script on someone's laptop has the
laptop's keys, can write anywhere, and leaves no record a controller can audit. On GurtOS the same agent:

| Production need | What GurtOS does |
|---|---|
| Least privilege | Each seat is its own Linux user with no sudo, its own copy of the files, and only its job's tools. Segregation of duties is tool access. |
| Secrets | CRS and Nessie keys live in a root-only vault. Tools that need them run in the box's core; the seats get results. |
| Human approval | The tool returns "needs controller"; the box pauses only that task and sends the question to the portal and phone. |
| Audit | One append-only, fsynced action log: every call, result, approval and cost. Decisions carry rule ids and CRS request ids. |
| Reliability | After a power cut the box rebuilds every task from the log and resumes it. Updates install to a second system slot and roll back if unhealthy. |
| Change control | The rules are a YAML file. Swap it per run with `{"rules": "/path/to/rules.yaml"}`; the tools refuse rule ids it does not have. |
| Regression testing | Every run is scored against an answer key from its log, so a prompt or rules change is measured, not guessed. |

```sh
curl -s -X POST localhost:7700/t/credit-desk/run -d '{"case": "fogline-2026-10"}'
```

## Limits

- The scorer checks decisions field by field, but a finding counts as caught by matching its rows, wording and rule
  ids, not by checking that its conclusion is right.
- Two collection steps differ from the key in every run: the clerk put two over-limit customers on credit hold
  (rule COL-3) instead of the aging step the key lists.
- The business credit file is simulated, and the bank is read, not written.
- The scored runs used a single controller seat on GurtOS Server. Routing by amount to different people is in GurtOS Business, shown here on a seeded box with scripted seats and sandbox data, not in a scored run.
- Sign-in with a work account is Entra-ready and tested with a standards test provider (forged tokens, a wrong audience and a replay are refused, and a user in no mapped group is refused), not with a real Entra or Google tenant. Signing out at the provider, and sign-in from the hosted app at app.gurtos.com, are not built either.
