## Why GurtOS, and what each part is for

**The problem.** A lender wants an agent to run identity and fraud checks, but on an ordinary operating system that agent holds the CRS login, can approve its own result and can pull any report it likes. Credit bureaus and lenders are what attackers go after (see the real attacks below), so no one lets an agent near that data.

**GurtOS Business is the environment built for it:** each seat is its own user without keys, approvals are routed by rules in plain words to the right person, every pull is in an append-only log, and one switch stops every agent. It runs on a computer the company owns, is light (one Node service, 4 GB of memory) and is open source under Apache-2.0. Business is on main; the scored runs were on GurtOS Server, and the screens are from a box seeded as the made-up Fogline Supply.

## Real attacks on financial companies

Credit bureaus are the target. Both incidents below are bureaus, which is the data this track uses. On an ordinary operating system an agent doing this work holds the keys, can approve its own work and can pull any report. Real attacks on financial companies show why that matters. Each row names its source and what was taken as the source states it, then says what GurtOS's controls do about that kind of attack and what they do not.

| Incident | What was taken, and how | Source | What GurtOS does about this kind of attack | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equifax, 2017 | About 147 million names and dates of birth, 145.5 million Social Security numbers and 209,000 payment card numbers with expiration dates. The FTC alleges Equifax did not fix a known flaw, kept administrator credentials in a plain-text file and did not segment its database servers. It agreed to pay at least $575 million. | [FTC, July 2019](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/07/equifax-pay-575-million-part-settlement-ftc-cfpb-states-related-2017-data-breach) | Keys sit in a root-only vault and never in a task's files or environment, and each task has its own Linux user and its own copy of the files. A task that is compromised has no credential to steal and cannot read another task's files. | GurtOS does not patch a company's own servers or databases, and it does not keep personal data out of a database you run. |
| TransUnion, July 2025 | Names, dates of birth and Social Security numbers of more than 4.4 million people, taken on July 28 from an outside application that held customer data for its U.S. consumer support. TransUnion says no credit information was accessed. The attacker is unknown and TransUnion declined to give more. | [TechCrunch, Aug 28, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/28/transunion-says-hackers-stole-4-4-million-customers-personal-information/) | A tool that talks to an outside service runs in the core with the key held there, and its task reaches only the hosts it is allowed. Anything large or unusual waits for a person's OK. | It does not stop a person from being talked into giving an outside party access. |

## How it works

<Flow steps={frontmatter.flow} />

| Check | CRS product and route |
|---|---|
| Credit report, each bureau | `/transunion/credit-report/standard/tu-prequal-fico9`, `/equifax/credit-report/standard/efx-prequal-vantage4`, `/experian/credit-report/standard/exp-prequal-vantage4` |
| Identity | `/transunion/identity-manager-verification/basic` |
| Fraud | `/fraud-finder/fraud-finder` (name, phone, email, address and the IP the application came from) |
| Public records | `/pp/fcrablj/data` (PitchPoint bankruptcies, liens, judgments) |
| Phone owner | `/accurate-append/reverse` |

**Responsible by rule.** The desk pulls only on owners who signed a personal guarantee and a credit check consent.
It pulls only what a trade-credit decision needs: no criminal or eviction searches (rule CHK-2). Only the controller
sees a fraud referral, and the applicant is told "We need to verify some information before we can offer terms",
never which check failed (rule FRR-3). The CRS client logs in once per box process and never retries a failed login.

**Read carefully.** Things the desk handles that CRS's sandbox does: credit reports return errors as HTTP 200, file
status says when a bureau has no file, Fraud Finder takes `ipAddress` (a field named `ip` is ignored), and a single
PitchPoint record comes back as an object rather than a list. `sandbox/crs.md` has the rest.

## Try it

On a box with `CRS_USERNAME` and `CRS_PASSWORD` in its vault:

```sh
curl -s -X POST localhost:7700/t/credit-desk/run -d '{"case": "fogline-2026-10", "seats": ["analyst"]}'
```

Or run the seven checks on their own, as root on a test VM, from a copy of `tracks/credit-desk`:

```sh
sudo node sandbox/crs-check.ts /tmp/crs-out cases/fogline-2026-10
```

To try another CRS persona, copy an application, paste a guarantor from `sandbox/crs-personas.json` (P05 subprime,
P12 identity mismatch, P14 deceased SSN, P16 fraud alert), check it with `check-case.ts`, and run it with
`{"dir": "/path/to/case"}`.

## Limits

- The sandbox returns results only for its 20 personas, so every guarantor is one of them.
- Business credit is simulated until the sandbox has a route for it.
- The desk reads CRS answers as summaries (scores, codes, signals). Full reports stay in CRS's retention, reachable
  by request id.
- Questions we have for CRS are in `sandbox/crs.md`, including one about every sandbox Experian tradeline carrying
  the same charge-off amount.
