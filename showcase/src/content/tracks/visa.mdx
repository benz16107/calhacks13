## One purchase, step by step

The same path a judge would follow, with what each step proves. Every payment here is a Stripe test-mode charge on a Visa test card, at our own stand-in store, the GurtOS Test Store.

**1. Say what you want.** In words, or fill the store, the price limit and the quantity. Claude proposes a signed mandate and cannot buy anything.

## Why this needs an operating system

On an ordinary system a shopping agent runs as you. The card, the mailbox login and the signing key are files and environment variables it can read, nothing separates "propose a purchase" from "make one", and nothing refunds it. On GurtOS each of those is the operating system's job, and the Shopper app only adds shopping on top. GurtOS Personal is the best fit: your own computer, even an old one, with the agent beside you on copies of your files.

## Real attacks on financial companies

A shopping agent holds money and card details, so it is a target for the same attacks. Each row names its source and what was taken as the source states it, then says what GurtOS's controls do about that kind of attack and what they do not.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equifax, 2017 | About 147 million names and dates of birth, 145.5 million Social Security numbers and 209,000 payment card numbers with expiration dates. <Steps title="Try it" time="About five minutes on a GurtOS computer. No real money moves.">

1. As root: `tracks/shopper/setup.sh`. It adds the Visa test card (`pm_card_visa`), a new Ed25519 signing key and a demo mailbox password to the vault, `/etc/box/vault.env`.

<Details summary="How a hijacked model still cannot spend your money">

- The model never holds a buy tool or the card. Droid gets `shopper.watch` (set a rule) and `shopper.propose` (suggest a reorder). Only purchase tasks the computer itself starts hold `shopper.buy`, and each one asks you first.
- Prices come from the store, not the model. `shopper.propose` takes SKUs and quantities and looks up the prices itself.
- Two gates on money: the tap, then the task's budget. A purchase above the budget stops and asks again.
- Power cuts do not pay twice. Each checkout carries the broker's stable call key as its Idempotency-Key, at the store and at Stripe.
- One proposal and one watch per agent task, so a looping agent cannot flood your phone.

**Visa Trusted Agent Protocol.** The computer signs every checkout the way Visa's sample agent does: an RFC 9421 HTTP message signature with the `sig2` label, Ed25519, `keyId`, `nonce`, `created`, `expires` (8 minutes) and the `agent-payer-auth` tag. One addition: it also signs `content-digest` (RFC 9530), so a different item, quantity or price fails the check. The computer publishes its public key at `GET /t/shopper/keys/box-shopper` in the shape of TAP's registry.

```
Signature-Input: sig2=("@authority" "@path" "content-digest"); created=1761271440; expires=1761271920;
                 keyId="box-shopper"; alg="ed25519"; nonce="..."; tag="agent-payer-auth"
Signature:       sig2=:...:
Content-Digest:  sha-256=:...:
```
