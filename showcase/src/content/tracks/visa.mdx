## How it works

<Flow steps={frontmatter.flow} />

**Three things it does.**

1. **Restock and price watcher.** The box checks a store on a schedule. When the item is in stock at or under your
   price, your phone asks: *Buy 2 × Hario V60 02 paper filters, 100 count for $13.00? $6.50 each, down from $8.99,
   under your $7.00 limit. TEST PAYMENT.*
2. **Household reorder.** Claude reads the receipts in the household's mailbox, works out what runs out this week, and
   proposes one reorder for your OK. It sees the receipts only: never the mailbox login, never your other email. The
   date arithmetic (how often an item is bought, when it runs out) is code; Claude does the judgment.
3. **Agents buying their own supplies.** A task pays for an API it needs with the wallet track's x402 payments, within
   its budget. See the [Coinbase](/tracks/coinbase/) page.

**Why a hijacked model cannot spend your money.**

- The model never holds a buy tool or the card. Droid gets `shopper.watch` (set a rule) and `shopper.propose`
  (suggest a reorder). Only purchase tasks the box itself starts hold `shopper.buy`, and each one asks you first.
- Prices come from the store, not the model. `shopper.propose` takes SKUs and quantities and looks up the prices
  itself.
- Two gates on money: the tap, then the task's budget. A purchase above the budget stops and asks again.
- Power cuts do not pay twice. Each checkout carries the broker's stable call key as its Idempotency-Key, at the store
  and at Stripe.
- One proposal and one watch per agent task, so a looping agent cannot flood your phone.

**Visa Trusted Agent Protocol.** The box signs every checkout the way Visa's sample agent does: an RFC 9421 HTTP
message signature with the `sig2` label, Ed25519, `keyId`, `nonce`, `created`, `expires` (8 minutes) and the
`agent-payer-auth` tag. One addition: it also signs `content-digest` (RFC 9530), so a different item, quantity or
price fails the check. The store refuses an unsigned order, a changed body, a replayed signature, an expired one, an
unknown key, and a signature made for another store. The box publishes its public key at
`GET /t/shopper/keys/box-shopper` in the shape of TAP's registry.

```
Signature-Input: sig2=("@authority" "@path" "content-digest"); created=1761271440; expires=1761271920;
                 keyId="box-shopper"; alg="ed25519"; nonce="…"; tag="agent-payer-auth"
Signature:       sig2=:…:
Content-Digest:  sha-256=:…:
```

## Try it

1. On a box, as root: `tracks/shopper/setup.sh`. It adds the Visa test card (`pm_card_visa`), a new Ed25519 signing
   key and a demo mailbox password to `/etc/box/vault.env`.
2. Optional: add `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY=sk_test_...` for real Stripe test-mode charges (the store refuses a live key), and
   `APIFY_TOKEN` for live Best Buy prices.
3. Open the Shopper app at `/t/shopper/` from the portal's app grid. Say "Get me the Hario V60 filters when they're
   under $7", or fill the four fields.
4. Open the GurtOS Test Store at `/t/shopper/store` and drop the price. The next check asks on your phone; approve it.
5. Undo the purchase task from the portal and watch the store refund the order.

Or run the whole flow: `./demo` (offline, simulated payments) or `DEMO_LIVE=shopper ./demo` (Stripe test mode,
Apify, Droid and devnet USDC).

## Limits

- A watch closes after one purchase, or when you deny it. Watch again for another.
- The running-low estimate assumes each purchase lasts until the next, whatever the quantity.
- The test store keeps price and stock changes in memory; they reset when the box restarts. Orders are on disk.
- The store remembers used nonces in memory. After a restart a captured signature could be sent again within its 8
  minutes, but its Idempotency-Key still returns the first order instead of charging twice.
- Visa's sample verifier only knows `@authority` and `@path`, so it would not accept our signatures, which also cover
  the body.
